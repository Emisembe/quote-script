Emisembe Writer for Google Docs – Complete Guide

Version: November 2026  ·  For: Murdock Emisembe (personal use)

This guide explains, step by step, how to install and use Emisembe Writer inside Google Docs. Emisembe Writer is a sidebar that can paraphrase, humanise and check the grammar of your text in English and German, at any language level from A1 to C2, and it can write in your own voice.

1. What the add-on does

The add-on opens as a panel (a "sidebar") on the right side of your Google Doc. You select text in your document, choose what you want to do, and the result appears in the sidebar. With one click you can put the result back into the document.

ToolWhat it does
ParaphraseRewrites your text in other words. 8 styles: Standard, Fluency, Formal / Business, Friendly, Simple, Academic, Shorter, Longer. You choose how much it should change (Light, Medium, Strong).
HumaniseMakes text sound like a real person wrote it: varied sentence length, plain words, no stock phrases or clichés. It keeps all your facts.
GrammarFinds spelling, grammar, punctuation and word-choice errors. Every error is underlined, with an explanation. You can apply fixes one by one or all at once.
Level (A1–C2)Makes the result fit a language level, for example simple A2 English or advanced C1 German. "Keep my level" leaves the level as it is.
Output languageSame language, English or Deutsch. If you choose the other language, the tool translates and rewrites in one step.
My voiceWrites the result in your personal style: direct, warm and structured. Works in English and German.

2. What you need before you start

  • A Google account (the one you use for Google Docs).
  • A Google Doc where you want to use the tool. Tip: create a new doc called "Writer – Template". You can copy it later for new work (see Part I).
  • The 4 files of the add-on: Code.gs, WriterCore.gs, Sidebar.html, appsscript.json. They are also inside the project zip, in the folders writer/google-docs/ and writer/core/ (writer-core.js = WriterCore.gs).
  • For Grammar: nothing else. Grammar works for free with LanguageTool, without any key.
  • For Paraphrase and Humanise: one AI engine. The easiest is a free Gemini key (Part A). Later you can use Claude (paid) or your own offline AI (Part H).

3. Part A – Get a free Gemini key

A "key" is like a password that lets the add-on use Google's AI for you. It is free; you only need your Google account.

  1. Open https://aistudio.google.com in your browser.
  2. Sign in with your Google account and accept the terms if asked.
  3. Click "Get API key" (on the left side or at the top).
  4. Click "Create API key". If it asks for a project, let it create one for you.
  5. A long code appears, usually starting with AIza…. Click Copy.
  6. Keep this key private. Do not post it or send it to other people. If someone gets it, delete it in AI Studio and create a new one.

Important: On the free tier, Google may use your text to improve its products. Do not use the free Gemini engine for private documents (for example legal papers, medical or personal details). For those, use Claude or the offline engine.

4. Part B – Install the add-on in a Google Doc

You only do this once per document (or once in your template document).

Step 1 – Open Apps Script

  1. Open your Google Doc (for example "Writer – Template").
  2. In the menu at the top, click Extensions → Apps Script. A new tab opens with the script editor.
  3. At the top left you see "Untitled project". Click it and rename it to Emisembe Writer, then click Rename. (This name appears later in your Extensions menu.)

Step 2 – Add the file Code.gs

  1. On the left, under Files, you already see a file called Code.gs. Click it.
  2. Select everything in the editor (Ctrl+A) and delete it.
  3. Open the file Code.gs from this project (with Notepad, or by opening it on your computer), copy everything (Ctrl+A, Ctrl+C) and paste it into the editor (Ctrl+V).
  4. Click the Save icon (or press Ctrl+S).

Step 3 – Add the file WriterCore

  1. Next to Files, click the + button and choose Script.
  2. Type the name WriterCore and press Enter (Apps Script adds ".gs" by itself).
  3. Delete the small example code inside it.
  4. Copy everything from WriterCore.gs (this is the same as writer-core.js) and paste it. Save.

Step 4 – Add the file Sidebar

  1. Click + next to Files and choose HTML.
  2. Type the name Sidebar exactly like this (capital S, no ".html") and press Enter.
  3. Delete the example code inside it.
  4. Copy everything from Sidebar.html and paste it. Save.

Step 5 – Replace appsscript.json

  1. On the far left, click the gear icon (Project Settings).
  2. Tick the box "Show 'appsscript.json' manifest file in editor".
  3. Click the < > (Editor) icon on the far left to go back to the files.
  4. Click the new file appsscript.json, delete everything inside, paste the content of appsscript.json from this project, and save.

Check: On the left you should now see 4 files: appsscript.json, Code.gs, WriterCore.gs, Sidebar.html. The names must be exactly like this.

5. Part C – First start and permissions

  1. Go back to the tab with your Google Doc and reload the page (press F5).
  2. Wait a few seconds. Click Extensions → Emisembe Writer → Open Writer.
  3. The first time, Google shows "Authorization required". Click Continue (or Review permissions) and choose your Google account.
  4. You will probably see "Google hasn't verified this app". This is normal, because you made this script yourself. Click Advanced, then Go to Emisembe Writer (unsafe).
  5. Google shows what the script may do: see and edit the current document, show a sidebar, and connect to external services (the AI). Click Allow.
  6. Click Extensions → Emisembe Writer → Open Writer again. The sidebar opens on the right.

Why these permissions? The script only works with the document you have open ("current document only"). It connects to the AI service you choose (Gemini, Claude) and to LanguageTool for grammar. It does not read your other files.

Add your Gemini key

  1. In the sidebar, at the bottom, click ⚙ Settings.
  2. Under AI engine → Use, choose Google Gemini — free tier.
  3. Paste your key into Gemini key.
  4. Click Find models. The list fills with the models your key can use. Choose a "flash" model (fast). If you are not sure, keep gemini-2.5-flash or the first one in the list.
  5. Click Save. At the bottom of the sidebar you now see "Gemini · (model name)".

7. Part E – How to use each tool

The basic workflow (for all tools)

  1. Select the text in your document with the mouse.
  2. In the sidebar, click ↓ Get selected text. The text appears in the box. (You can also type or paste text directly into the box.)
  3. Choose the tool at the top: Paraphrase, Humanise or Grammar.
  4. Choose the options (Style, Level, Output, Change, My voice).
  5. Click the big button (Rephrase, Humanise or Check grammar). Wait a few seconds.
  6. Read the result. Changed words are highlighted in yellow.
  7. Click Replace selection to put the result in place of the selected text, or Insert below to add it under the selected text (your original stays). Copy copies the result so you can paste it anywhere.

Tip: Before you click "Replace selection", make sure the same text is still selected in the document. If you clicked somewhere else, select it again. If you are unsure, use Insert below – it never deletes anything. And remember: Ctrl+Z in Google Docs undoes a replacement.

Paraphrase

StyleUse it for
StandardNew wording, same tone and meaning.
FluencyFix awkward sentences, make it read smoothly, change as little as possible.
Formal / BusinessEmails to companies, offices (Behörden), customers, applications.
FriendlyWarm messages to colleagues and friends.
SimpleMake text easy to understand (short sentences, common words).
AcademicReports, essays, research texts.
ShorterCut the text by about 30–50%, keep all important points.
LongerExplain the same points more fully (no new facts are added).

Change: Light keeps most of your words. Medium rephrases freely. Strong may rebuild whole sentences.

Rough drafts and voice notes: If your text is spoken-style (like a voice message without punctuation), Paraphrase turns it into clean written text. Fluency + Light is good for this.

Humanise

Use Humanise when a text sounds stiff, robotic or "too perfect". It varies sentence length, uses contractions in English (I'm, don't), removes phrases like "I hope this message finds you well" or "in today's fast-paced world", and keeps every fact, name and number. Combine it with My voice to make it sound like you.

Honest note: Humanise makes text more natural, but nobody can guarantee that "AI detectors" will not flag it. Detectors are unreliable.

Grammar

  1. Choose Grammar at the top. Under Text language choose Auto-detect, English or Deutsch.
  2. Choose the Level: it decides how simple the explanations are (only with the AI checker).
  3. Click Check grammar.
  4. Errors are underlined in red. Below the text, every error has a card: the wrong words (crossed out), the correction (green) and an explanation.
  5. Click Apply to fix one error, or Ignore if you want to keep it. Apply all fixes everything at once. Fixed parts turn green.
  6. Click Replace selection to put the corrected text into your document.

Translating while rewriting

Set Output to English or Deutsch. Example: write a rough English note, choose Paraphrase + Formal + Output Deutsch + Level B2, and you get a clean, formal German version.

The levels follow the official CEFR descriptions. They are a strong guide for the AI, not a certified test result.

9. Part G – Setting up "My voice"

When My voice is ticked, the AI writes like you. Your profile is already filled in with your style: Direct. Warm. Unfiltered. – structure first, a warm opener, candid questions, signposting ("The point is…", "In my view…"), respectful framing ("I stand to be corrected"), at most one proverb, and none of the typical AI phrases.

Make it better

  1. Open ⚙ Settings → My voice.
  2. In Samples of my writing, paste 3 to 6 real texts you wrote yourself: emails, messages, a short letter. Use both English and German texts, so it learns your German voice too.
  3. Remove anything very private from the samples (names, addresses, case numbers).
  4. Lines that start with # are notes for you – they are never sent to the AI.
  5. You can also edit How I write. Write simple rules, one per line, for example: "I always end emails with a clear next step."
  6. Click Save.

Note: A single setting can hold about 8,500 characters, so keep samples short and typical.

10. Part H – Switching AI engines

Gemini (free) – what you use now

Free with daily limits. If you see "You reached the Gemini free limit", wait a few minutes (per-minute limit) or until tomorrow (daily limit), or choose a lighter model with Find models.

Claude (paid) – when you can afford it

  1. Open https://console.anthropic.com and create an account.
  2. Go to Billing and add credit (for example $5 – that is enough for hundreds of paragraphs).
  3. Set a monthly spending limit in the console, so you can never spend more than you want.
  4. Go to API Keys → Create Key and copy the key (starts with sk-ant-).
  5. In the sidebar: ⚙ Settings → Use: Claude, paste the key, choose the model, Save.

Approximate cost per paragraph (~150 words): Opus 5.5 about 1–2 US cents, Sonnet 5.5 under 1 cent, Haiku 5.5 a fraction of a cent (prices as of late 2026 – check the console).

Offline AI on your PC (experimental in Google Docs)

  1. First set up Ollama on your PC with the desktop guide ("Emisembe Writer – Desktop App – Complete Guide"). You need about 8 GB free disk space.
  2. In the sidebar: ⚙ Settings → Use: Offline AI on my PC (Ollama).
  3. Check Ollama address (http://localhost:11434) and Offline model (for example gemma3:4b).
  4. Click Test connection. If Chrome asks "allow access to devices on your local network", click Allow.
  5. If you see "✓ Connected", click Save and use the tools as normal (it is slower than the cloud).
  6. If it says "Not reachable", Chrome is probably blocking Google Docs from talking to your PC. In that case, use the desktop app for offline work and copy the result into Google Docs.

11. Part I – Using the add-on in other documents

The script belongs to the document where you installed it. Ways to use it in more documents:

  • Template (easiest): Install it in "Writer – Template". For new work, open the template and click File → Make a copy. The copy has the sidebar too. Your settings and keys are already saved in your account.
  • Existing document: Open the template with the sidebar, paste your text there, edit it, then copy it back. Or use the desktop app with copy and paste.
  • Real add-on for all documents: possible later, but it needs a Google Cloud project and a review by Google. Ask Claude for help when you want this.

12. Part J – Updating to a new version

  1. Open the document → Extensions → Apps Script.
  2. For each changed file, select all, delete and paste the new content. Save.
  3. Reload the document (F5). Your settings and keys stay saved.

14. Part L – Privacy and costs

  • Gemini free tier: free; your text is sent to Google and may be used to improve Google's products.
  • Claude: paid per use; your text is sent to Anthropic to be processed.
  • LanguageTool online: free; your text is sent to languagetool.org.
  • Offline (Ollama): free; your text stays on your PC.
  • Your keys are stored in your Google account's private script settings, not in the document.
