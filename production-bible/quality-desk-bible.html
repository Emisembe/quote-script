Your production bible for LinkedIn video

This is your own system for planning, filming, editing and publishing short videos that show what you know about quality management. It uses the structure of the River Musa guide as a model. The brand, content and rules here are built from your two CVs, your certificates and your Wayfair reference letter.

Brand idea

The audit trail

River Musa used the riverbank as its metaphor. Yours is the audit trail: every claim has evidence. On screen, each fact gets a source badge. In scripts, each opinion is tied to something you did, read or measured. This is a habit your target audience already respects.

On camera

Film mainly in English, where your delivery is strongest (C2). Burn in German captions or write the post text in German, so German hiring managers can follow.

German series

Film one short piece a week in German (QM-Wort der Woche, 30–45 s). It shows German-market recruiters your progress towards C1 in a format they can judge for themselves.

Register

In German content, use Sie. The Mittelstand QM audience is conservative, and Sie is never wrong in a hiring context. In English, keep it conversational: "you", with contractions.

QD-PB-05Rev. 1.0Hooks EN + DE · CTAs · connecting phrases

Hooks & CTAs

You have about three seconds on LinkedIn before the thumb moves on. There's no "Hi everyone, today I want to talk about…". The first sentence is the hook, and it has to work on mute, so the hook text also appears on screen.

Hook formulas

Hook rules for a professional audience

Specific beats clever

"Clause 7.5.3" or "−15 %" earns more trust than "a big improvement". A specific detail tells the viewer you did the work.

Loss framing, honestly

"The audit finding most companies get for the same clause" works because people fear losing more than they want to gain. It has to be true and specific. A vague threat reads as clickbait to this audience.

Open one loop

Promise an answer in the first 5 seconds ("…and the fix takes ten minutes") and deliver it before the CTA. Leave exactly one loop open at the end: next week's topic.

Never punch down

Don't mock former employers, auditors or colleagues. Recruiters read your tone as a preview of how you'd talk about their company.

CTAs (one per video)

Connecting phrases

Hook → point
  • "Here's what that means in an audit."
  • "Let me show you on paper."
  • "The standard says one thing. The floor says another."
  • DE: „Was heißt das konkret im Audit?"
Between points
  • "Second thing."
  • "Now, the part most people skip."
  • "This is where it usually breaks."
  • DE: „Und jetzt der Teil, den viele übersehen."
Building to the turn
  • "We fixed it. Three weeks later it was back."
  • "That's when I stopped treating the symptom."
  • "The data said something different."
  • DE: „Dann haben wir die Ursache gesucht, nicht den Schuldigen."
Closing the loop
  • "So, back to the question from the start."
  • "That's the evidence an auditor wants."
  • "One sheet. Eight steps. Done."
  • DE: „Das ist der Nachweis, den der Auditor sehen will."
QD-PB-06Rev. 1.0From idea to script · adapted from River Musa Story Lab

Story Lab

A topic isn't a story. "ISO 9001 clause 10.2" is a topic. "The same nonconformity came back three times, until we asked a different question" is a story. People watch stories, and recruiters remember them.

Where your stories come from

Source 1 · Observation

Something you saw on the floor or in an audit that surprised you.

"Every pallet label was correct. The scanner said otherwise."

Source 2 · Tension

The gap between what the standard asks for and what happens on the floor.

"The procedure said sign off daily. Nobody could, because the form was in the office."

Source 3 · The unanswered question

Something you had to look up yourself. Your audience has the same question.

"What's the actual difference between a correction and a corrective action?"

Source 4 · Contrast

The same standard in two sectors, logistics against recycling against social care, or two ways of working, Nairobi against Hessen.

"ISO 9001 at a plastics recycler vs. a warehouse: same clause, different evidence."

The four parts every story needs

PartQuestionQuality Desk example
CharacterWho or what is this about?You as a new quality auditor; or a process, a supplier, a form
ProblemWhat goes wrong?The same nonconformity keeps coming back after every correction
StakesWhy does it matter?Returns, delays, customer complaints, a finding at the next audit
ChangeWhat's different at the end?Root cause found, SOP changed, error rate down 15 %

If one part is missing, you have a lecture, not a story. Check all four before you write a single line.

Five structures

Type 1 · The discovery

Question → what I expected → what I found → what it means → what you can do.

Best for P1 and P6. "I thought ISO 14001 was about recycling bins. Clause 6.1.2 is about something else."

Type 2 · The fix

Symptom → first (wrong) fix → symptom returns → root cause → lasting fix + number.

Best for P3. Your −15 % and −30 % stories use this structure.

Type 3 · The system

What people think it is → layer 1: what it asks → layer 2: what evidence proves it → layer 3: where it fails → what to do.

Best for P1 and P4. It suits LinkedIn and YouTube search well.

Type 4 · Myth vs. requirement

The myth → "FINDING" stamp → what the standard actually says → the practical version.

Best for P1 and P2. "ISO 9001 requires a quality manual." It hasn't since 2015.

Type 5 · The bridge

Where I started → the step that changed things → what it took → what I'd tell someone starting now.

Best for P6. Always told looking forward.

Writing for the ear

Rules
  • One idea per sentence.
  • Active voice: "We changed the SOP", not "The SOP was changed".
  • Use numbers, not adjectives.
  • Use contractions in English.
  • Say the key point twice, in different words.
  • Read it out loud. If you stumble on a line, rewrite it.

Delivery notes

Opening

The first word is on frame one. Relax your jaw before you start. In an ECU every tension shows. Say the line quietly: quiet reads as confidence.

The number

"Fifteen percent" is the one line people should remember. Slow down, leave a micro-pause before it, and duck the music to ~5 %. Emphasis comes from space, not volume.

The CTA

Drop your energy slightly before "If you work in quality", then lift on the question. The push-in does the emotional work, so keep your voice steady.

German take

Film the German version as its own take, not a dub. Slow down by 10 %. If a word catches, pause, breathe and repeat the whole sentence. Cutting on sentence boundaries is invisible.

QD-PB-09Rev. 1.0Home desk studio · phone or GoPro · no extra gear


Studio & Camera


Your set is the audit desk. Build it once, photograph it so you can rebuild it the same way, and film every talking-head video there. A consistent background is what makes 50 videos look like one channel.



The desk set




Behind you
  • A plain wall, lit by one warm lamp pointed at the wall. This adds depth.
  • One shelf with 2–3 binders, spines reading "QM-Handbuch", "Audits 2026", "CAPA". Label your own empty binders.
  • One small Audit Blue object and one yellow highlighter in view: quiet brand consistency.


In front of you
  • Window light on your face. Overcast is best.
  • Your face must be the brightest thing in the frame. If the window is behind you, turn the set around.
  • Before every take, remove the one most distracting object.


Props that tell the story
  • A whiteboard or A3 pad for PDCA, Ishikawa and 8D drawings
  • A clipboard with your own mock checklist
  • A caliper or ruler for the Prüfmittel metaphor (about €10)
  • Sticky notes in blue and yellow





Framing




Eyes on the upper third
Turn on the grid. Your eyes sit on the top grid line, never in the centre. Leave a little headroom, not a ceiling.


Camera height
At eye level or 2–3 cm below it. For professional content, the slightly low angle reads as grounded authority. Never shoot from above.


Look at the lens
Put a small yellow sticker next to the lens and put the teleprompter text as close to the lens as you can. The lens is the recruiter.


Hands in frame
Frame from mid-chest so your hand gestures show on the key words. For vertical video, keep your whole head inside the safe zone.

QD-PB-10Rev. 1.0Focusrite Scarlett 2i2 · Audacity 01MRC · Camtasia mix


Audio


Viewers forgive soft video. They don't forgive bad sound. With a professional audience, clean voice audio says "this person is thorough" before you've said anything useful.


Recording




Signal chain
Mic → Scarlett 2i2 → laptop. Record the voice as a separate WAV (48 kHz, 24-bit) alongside the phone video. Clap once at the start to sync the two.


Gain
Speak your loudest line. Set the gain so the Scarlett halo stays green and peaks land around −12 to −6 dBFS. It should never turn red.


Room
Soft furnishings help: curtains, a sofa, a rug. Record 10 seconds of room tone before every session so you have silence to patch gaps in the edit.


Checks
Fridge, heating, open window, phone notifications off. Listen back on headphones before you start the real take.




Processing




1AudacityRun the 01MRC Best EQ Settings macro on the voice WAV. That's your settled preset. Don't tweak it further.


2CamtasiaImport the processed WAV and align it to the clap. Mute the phone's own audio track.


3Mix in CamtasiaUse volume keyframes for music and ducking, not Audacity.


4Export checkAim for about −14 LUFS integrated, with true peaks below −1 dB.

QD-PB-13Rev. 1.0Camtasia workflow · cutting rules · look · captions


Edit Guide


Cut in a fixed order and don't go back a step. Most time lost in editing comes from polishing graphics on a cut that isn't final yet.



The Camtasia workflow




1 · ORGANISEProject folderYYYY-MM-DD_QD-EpNN_title with subfolders A-roll / B-roll / Audio / GFX / Export.


2 · SYNCVoice WAVAlign it to the clap, mute the phone audio, apply the 01MRC-processed track.


3 · ROUGH CUTA-roll onlyKeep the best take of each line and cut restarts. Aim for about 70 % of the fillers removed.


4 · PACE CUTTightenClose gaps down to about 0.15 s. Silence removal first, then fix by hand.


5 · B-ROLLTrack 2Cut the B-roll in on the key verb. Keep the voice running underneath.


6 · LOOKColourWhite balance → exposure → contrast. Push-in on static shots.


7 · CAPTIONSBurn inGenerate automatically, correct every word, then highlight one word per sentence.


8 · GRAPHICSTrack 3Lower third, clause badge, stat, source badge, end card.


9 · MIX & EXPORTLevelsDuck the music, check against −14 LUFS, export per platform.





Ep. 01 on the timeline




Track 1 is A-roll (blue), track 2 B-roll (green), track 3 graphics (yellow and dark), track 4 music (grey). The B-roll never covers the hook, the number or the CTA: those moments need your face.





Cutting rules




Pace
Short-form: something changes every 3–4 seconds, whether a cut, a B-roll clip, a graphic or a push-in. No single static angle for longer than 4 seconds, and no talking head longer than 12.


Face moments
Always cut back to your face for the hook, the number, the turn and the CTA. Viewers follow a person, not a scene.


Cut on the verb
B-roll comes in on the action word: "we changed the SOP" → cut to the pen editing the document.


J- and L-cuts
Bring the next shot's audio in about 0.5 s early, or let the previous one linger about 0.5 s. This makes cuts feel seamless.


Transitions
Hard cuts, 95 % of the time. A dip-to-black only before the end card. No spins, wipes or page curls: a quality audience reads them as sloppy.


Length discipline
If a line doesn't serve the one-sentence test, cut it. A 55-second video that holds attention beats a 90-second one that doesn't.





The look




Base grade (every clip)
  1. White balance: skin looks natural, the wall neutral grey.
  2. Exposure: the face is the brightest area.
  3. Brightness/Contrast +5 to +10.
  4. Saturation −5. Calm, not cold.


No stylised LUT
River Musa uses Milo 5 for a cinematic travel look. The Quality Desk stays clean and neutral. If you want a LUT, pick one subtle one at 20–30 % and never change it.


Movement
Add a slow 5–8 % push-in on almost every static shot. It's the cheapest way to make a phone video look produced. Stabilise handheld B-roll one notch.





Captions




Why
Most LinkedIn video autoplays on mute. The often-quoted "85 % watch muted" comes from a 2016 Facebook study. The exact figure is old, but the behaviour isn't. The hook has to work silently.


Style
Archivo 800, white with a 3 px graphite stroke, 3–6 words per card, centred just above the bottom safe line. One word per sentence on a highlighter block.


Two layers
Burn in the captions, and upload an .srt file on LinkedIn so the text is searchable and accessible. For the English video, burn in English and upload a German .srt, or the other way round.

QD-PB-19Rev. 1.0German legal basics · privacy · confidentiality · not legal advice


Rules & Boundaries


You work in compliance, so your channel has to be compliant too. A single slip here would undo the credibility the videos are meant to build. These are the practical basics. When money or a business is involved, check with the IHK or a tax adviser.



What never goes on camera




Never
  • Residence permit, passport, ID numbers, date of birth
  • Arbeitszeugnisse, bank details, salary documents
  • Home address or street views that identify it
  • Real client, employer or supplier documents, audit reports or internal KPIs
  • Colleagues or guests without written consent


Always fine
  • Your knowledge and opinions
  • Mock documents you made yourself
  • Facts already on your CV (the Proof Bank)
  • Public, published information, with its source named
  • Your desk, your city, your learning journey


Ask first
  • Naming a former employer in a critical context. Don't do it.
  • Filming inside any company, even a friend's
  • Once you're employed, check the employer's social media policy before posting about work topics
